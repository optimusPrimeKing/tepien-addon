# Structured Observation Protocol
## Exhibition "No to Genocide!"
### Multimodal Discourse Analysis after Kress and van Leeuwen (2001; 2006)

---

## BLOCK 0. GENERAL SPATIAL ORGANISATION

*Completed once — on entering and at the end of the visit.*

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Route | Linear or free? Branches? | Linear, directed. Four consecutive halls without branches. Movement strictly forward along the walls with LED screens. |
| Light regime | Dark / light, transitions | Full darkening along the entire route. Spot lighting of the screens and banners. The contrast of dark space and bright screens creates an immersive effect. |
| Layout | Where the screens are located | LED screens — along the perimeter of the halls along the route. In front of each LED screen there is a duplicating sensor table. In the centre of the first hall — a long sensor table (flags → forget-me-nots). Banners with quotations — at the entrance, on both sides of the passageway. |
| Soundscape | Background sound, music, silence | Continuous background music — an Armenian melody dedicated to the genocide. No additional sound effects. The music creates a unified affective background for the entire route. |
| Transitions | Screen changes, spatial thresholds | Every few seconds all LED screens switch synchronously to a dark screen with a symbol (forget-me-not or thorn crown) and the title of the next theme — a visual pause between sections. |
| Number of elements | Thematic screens, tables, banners | 11 thematic LED screens (some of them paired by theme), one sensor table at the entrance, duplicating sensor tables in front of each LED screen, a series of 6 banners at the entrance, 1 screen with the symbolism of the exhibition. |

# ANALYSIS OF INDIVIDUAL ELEMENTS

## Element 1: Entrance banners (quotations from authoritative figures)

**Type of element:** Banners (a series of ~6 vertical banners on dark walls).

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo, text, graphics | Monochrome / sepia portraits of historical and contemporary figures + their quotations about the Armenian genocide. Name and position at the bottom of the banner. |
| Participants | Victims, witnesses, historical figures | Authoritative declarative witnesses: Pope John Paul II, V. V. Putin, Catholicos Vazgen I, Fridtjof Nansen, Pope Francis, Anatole France. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | State — static official portraits. The narrative is produced by the quotation, not by the image. |
| Language of the text | Documentary, emotional, neutral | Rhetorical and declarative register. The quotations contain evaluative judgements ("prelude to horrors", "mournful date", "unheard-of tragedies"). |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Direct gaze (demand) / sideways (offer) | Gaze not at the camera on most portraits — offer. Exception: Putin (direct gaze — demand of solidarity). |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Medium shot (portrait to the chest / shoulders). Creates an official but not intimate distance. |
| Modality | Realism — archival photo vs graphics | High modality — real photographs of people. Sepia / monochrome slightly reduces naturalism, adds solemnity. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left (known) / right (new) | Portrait — at the top (the already-known figure as given). Quotation — below / to the right (new — this figure's statement about the genocide). |
| Ideal / real | Top (symbol) / bottom (fact) | Ideal — the portrait (authority, symbolic figure). Real — the quotation (a concrete utterance). Name and position — at the bottom, as a caption to the real. |
| Salience | What dominates — size, colour, contrast | The quotations dominate — large font in the centre of the banner. The portrait occupies the upper third. The dark background of the wall amplifies the contrast of the banner. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image — same or different | Full coherence: the portrait legitimates the quotation. The authority of the face works as a visual signature under the words. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual / sound / space | Text (quotation) — content dominant. Visual (portrait) — authorising dominant. They work together. |
| Interactivity | Requires physical action | Passive. The viewer reads and looks. The very placement — on both sides of the passageway — forms a corridor of authoritative voices, through which the visitor has to physically pass. |

## Element 2: LED screen "Contents"

**Type of element:** LED screen (informational, navigational).

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo, text, infographics | Infographics: numbered themes (1–11) on a red-and-white background with geometric shapes. No photographs. |
| Participants | Victims, witnesses, symbols, figures | None. Only structural information. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | State. A static scheme. |
| Language of the text | Documentary, emotional, neutral | Neutral informational: a list of themes in chronological order from 1877 to "The World Recognises and Condemns". |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | No gaze. An abstract scheme. |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Long shot — the entire scheme is taken in by the eye at once. |
| Modality | Realism | Low — a purely graphic scheme. Geometric design, not a representation of reality. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | The left side — earlier themes (given, known history); the right — later themes (new for the viewer). Themes 1–7 on the left, 8–11 on the right. |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | "Contents" — large at the top (ideal — the promise of a narrative). The concrete themes — below (real — what will be shown). |
| Salience | What dominates | Large numerals 1–11 scattered across the screen in different sizes. The red colour of the geometric shapes. The word "CONTENTS" — in the largest typeface. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Only typography and colour — they are coherent. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual / sound | Typography. Numerals function as a visual route map. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | Passive. The function is to orient the viewer in the structure of the narrative before the route begins. |

## Element 3: Entrance sensor table (flags → forget-me-nots)

**Type of element:** Interactive sensor table.

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo, graphics, symbol | Digital graphics: the flags of Russia and Armenia → at the touch, the surface fills with forget-me-nots (blue / violet flowers on a light-blue background). On the screen: an instruction — "wave your hand". |
| Participants | Victims, witnesses, symbols | No people. Flags = the states that are participants in the history. Forget-me-nots = the (official) symbol of the genocide. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | Action — the transformation of the image under the viewer's hand. The viewer themselves produces the change of image. |
| Language of the text | Register | Instructive ("wave your hand"). Minimal. |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | No gaze. But the instruction is a direct address to the viewer ("wave"). |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Looks downward at a horizontal surface. A non-standard angle — the table as a stage. |
| Modality | Realism | High — bright, saturated colours of the flags. The forget-me-nots are stylised but recognisable. The digital animation reduces documentary character but amplifies symbolism. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right / transformation | Flags = given (states, history). Forget-me-nots = new (the result of the touch, the symbol of memory). The transformation occurs in the moment of interaction. |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | Not applicable in the standard sense — a horizontal surface. The entire space of the table = a single field of meaning. |
| Salience | What dominates | Before the touch: the colours of the flags (red, blue, orange of Armenia; white, blue, red of Russia). After: a blue field of forget-me-nots occupies the entire table. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Coherence through action: the instruction + the transformation of the image work together. The viewer becomes the "author" of the change of narrative — from the political (flags) to the memorial (forget-me-nots). |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual / space | Tactile + visual. The physical action is dominant. This is the only element of the exhibition in which the viewer literally "creates" the image of memory. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | Active (obligatory). The content is fully revealed only at the touch. The transformation from flags to forget-me-nots = a curatorially staged gesture of "turning the political into the memorial". |

## Element 4: LED screen "Symbolism of the exhibition and its meanings"

**Type of element:** LED screen (explanatory).

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Graphics, text | Black background. White graphic symbols (wings, thorn crown) + textual explanations. No photographs. |
| Participants | Symbols | No people. Wings = "emancipation, freedom, recognition of the genocide". Thorn crown = "martyrdom, mass exterminations". |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | State — static symbols. |
| Language of the text | Register | Declarative: the curator directly explains to the viewer how to read the images of the exhibition. Metatext — an instruction for interpretation. |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | No gaze — abstract symbols. |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Long shot — the symbols occupy the entire screen. |
| Modality | Realism | Low — stylised black-and-white graphics. The deliberate abstraction strengthens the universal, not historically concrete character of the symbols. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | Symbol on the left / in the centre (given — visual sign). The textual explanation on the right (new — the meaning that has to be learned). |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | Wings (above) = ideal (freedom, liberation). Thorn crown (below) = real (suffering, martyrdom). |
| Salience | What dominates | Sharp contrast of white on black. The wings are a more large-scale symbol and dominate visually. The thorn crown is centrally and geometrically accentuated. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Full: the image of the symbol + its textual decoding. The curator leaves no room for an alternative interpretation — the meaning is set explicitly. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual | Text (the explanation) dominates in content. The visual is an illustration of the text. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | Passive. The function is a semiotic instruction: the viewer is taught to "read" the exhibition before it begins. |

## Element 5: LED screen "The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878 and the Armenian question" + sensor table

**Type of element:** LED screen + duplicating sensor table.

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo, map, text, engraving | Archival black-and-white photographs (refugees, military scenes), a historical engraving, maps of territories (the Near East, the cartography of Greek and Roman sources), a portrait of Mkrtich Khrimian, a photograph of the Berlin Congress of 1878. |
| Participants | Victims, figures, witnesses | Refugees (victims — anonymous, in movement). Historical figures: Mkrtich Khrimian. Diplomatic scenes: the Berlin Congress. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | Mixed: refugees in movement (narrative action — flight), cavalry in attack (action — war). Official scenes — state (negotiations). |
| Language of the text | Register | Documentary-historical. A neutral academic tone with elements of a narrative of suffering ("about 2.5 million Armenians, who were subjected to discrimination"). |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | Predominantly offer — refugees and military scenes look "past". Khrimian — direct gaze (demand — the legitimacy of the witness). |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Medium and long shots — emphasise the scale of the events, not the individual fate. |
| Modality | Realism | High for archival photographs. Medium for engravings (documentary illustrations by contemporaries). The maps — abstract but historically authoritative. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | Left — images of suffering, refugees (given — the known fact of victimhood). Right — diplomatic documents, maps (new — the political context, the responsibility of the powers). |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | Typographic dates 1877–1878 at the top as the ideal (chronological frame, historical status). Photographs of refugees and engravings below — the real (documentation). |
| Salience | What dominates | Large red typographic dates 1877 and 1878. Red geometric "cuts" over the photographs. Large white text of the heading "The Russo-Turkish War…". |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Coherence: the text explains the photograph, the photograph illustrates the text. The map adds a spatial argument to the historical narrative. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual | Visual (archival photograph) is emotionally dominant. Text — informational dominant. Dates — structural dominant. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | The duplicating sensor table — active (the viewer chooses a section to study). But the choice is limited to the predefined sections. The LED screen — passive. The duplication reduces the necessity of interaction. |

## Element 6: LED screen "The massacre of the Armenians in the Ottoman Empire 1894–1896"

**Type of element:** LED screen.

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Historical engraving, text | Engravings from the European press of 1894–1896 — scenes of violence (pogroms, killings). Red overlay on the black-and-white images. No photographs. |
| Participants | Victims, witnesses | Victims — Armenians (anonymous, in scenes of violence). Perpetrators — Turkish troops, Kurdish militias (identified in the text, not individualised in the image). |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | Action — scenes of pogroms, attacks. Dynamic scenes. Movement — flight of victims and attack. |
| Language of the text | Register | Documentary-factual: dates, numbers of the killed. Emotional emphasis through concrete detail ("on that day about 2 thousand Armenians were killed", "about 10 thousand people"). |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | Offer — the engraved scenes are massive and impersonal. The victims do not look at the viewer. |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Long shot — scenes of streets, crowds. The scale of victimhood is conveyed through numbers, not through a close-up of individual suffering. |
| Modality | Realism | Medium — the engraving as a 19th-century medium. The curator explicitly indicates: "based on eyewitness testimony". The red overlay — an additional coding layer that strengthens blood and violence. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | Left — scenes of violence (given — the fact of the pogrom). Right — chronological development (new — the next stage of repression). |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | The heading "The massacre of the Armenians in the Ottoman Empire 1894–1896" — centred as the ideal (the official historical qualification). The engravings — above and below (the real — the visualisation of the qualification). |
| Salience | What dominates | The red overlay on the engravings is the strongest visual dominant. The red literally "colours" black-and-white history in the colour of blood. A large central heading. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Coherence: the text names what the image shows. The red overlay — a third mode that reinforces both. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual | Visual (engravings + red overlay). Colour — a separate semiotic layer. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | Passive. |

| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Medium shot for portraits. Long shot for caricatures. |

| Category | What to record | Observation |

| Category | What to record | Observation |

## Element 9: LED screen "The Russian soldier — eyewitness of the genocide"

**Type of element:** LED screen + duplicating sensor table.

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo, military map, documents | Archival photographs — the consequences of the genocide: mutilated bodies of Armenians, burned houses. A military map of the Caucasian front of 1914–1918 (Zayonchkovsky). Photographs of survivors in Trebizond. |
| Participants | Victims, witnesses | Victims — the bodies of killed Armenians (anonymous, documentary). Witnesses — Russian soldiers (their letters, not their faces). The role of the eyewitness is central to the narrative of this screen. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | State — the bodies of the dead. This is a principled choice: not the action (killing) but its result. A stopped moment — documentation. |
| Language of the text | Register | Documentary-testimonial: "About the events on the Turkish front they wrote, describing the horrors committed by the Turks. These letters became the first eyewitness testimonies of the Genocide of the Armenian people in Turkey." |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | Offer — the bodies of the dead, the ruins. None of those depicted looks at the viewer. The viewer's gaze — that of an investigator / witness. |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Medium and long shots. The camera does not move into close-up — it preserves a documentary distance, does not exploit. |
| Modality | Realism | Highest — documentary photographs of the consequences. The map — abstract but with an authoritative attribution. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | Left — photographs of the consequences (given — the fact of the killings). Right — the military map (new — the spatial scale of the operation). |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | Typographic dates 1914–1916 — at the top as the ideal (the historical frame). The photographs of bodies — below as the real (proof). |
| Salience | What dominates | The heading "The Russian soldier — eyewitness of the genocide" — in the largest typeface. The photographs of bodies — the maximum affective load. Red in the dates. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Coherence: the soldiers' letters + the photographs document the same thing. Testimony is doubled — text and image confirm each other. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual | Visual (documentary photographs of the consequences) — emotionally and evidentially dominant. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | The sensor table duplicates. Passive by default. |

## Element 10: LED screen "Crime against humanity and civilisation"

**Type of element:** LED screen.

### Block 1 — Representation

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Type of content | Archival photo | Exclusively archival photographs by Armin Wegner and Viktor Pichmann: bodies of those who perished from hunger and deprivation, the deported, children in the concentration settlements (Ras al-Ayn, Deir ez-Zor). |
| Participants | Victims | Only victims: the dead, the exhausted, the children. There are no historical figures, no witnesses in the photographs — only victims. The maximum concentration of victimhood. |
| Narrative processes | Action or state | A state of death and exhaustion. Bodies, the frozen poses of dying children. A visual fixation of stopped life. |
| Language of the text | Register | Documentary-juridical: "On 24 May 1915 Great Britain, France and Russia issued a joint declaration condemning the massacre…". Lemkin is cited (the definition of genocide as the destruction of culture). The register is accusatory. |

### Block 2 — Interaction

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Demand / offer | Direct gaze — in some children in the photographs (demand). This is the most affective moment of the whole exhibition: a child looks directly at the viewer through a hundred years. |
| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Close-up — the children. Long shot — fields of bodies. A change of scale from the individual to the mass. |
| Modality | Realism | Maximum — professional documentary photographs with attribution (Wegner, Pichmann). This is the "evidentiary" level of the narrative. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Given / new | Left / right | Left — organisational documents (orders for extermination). Right — photographs of the consequences. The narrative logic: order → consequence. |
| Ideal / real | Top / bottom | The heading "Crime against Humanity" — at the bottom left (non-standard — not an ideal but a verdict). The photographs occupy most of the space — the real dominates over the symbolic. |
| Salience | What dominates | The photographs of children — the maximum affective salience. The red background behind the dates 1914–1915. The heading in quotation marks reinforces the citational (juridical) character. |

### Block 4 — Multimodal synthesis

| Category | What to record | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence of modes | Text and image | Full coherence: juridical definition + photographic evidence + international reaction. Three levels of proof. |
| Dominant mode | Text / visual | Visual (photographs of victims) is absolutely dominant. This is the emotional peak of the exhibition. |
| Interactivity | Physical action | Passive. Deliberately — the viewer does not need to "do" anything other than look. |

| Distance | Close / medium / long shot | Close-up — the child portrait (proximity). Long shot — the mass of refugees (the scale of tragedy). |
| Modality | Realism | High — dated documentary photographs. |

### Block 3 — Composition

| Category | What to record | Observation |
